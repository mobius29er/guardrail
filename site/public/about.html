The project

Why a halligan

A halligan bar is a firefighter's forcible-entry tool — adze, pick and fork on one shaft, made for getting through a door that is supposed to hold. This is the same idea pointed at an AI assistant's guardrails: you find out what holds by attacking it deliberately, not by hoping.

The project started from a real red-team session against a Catholic AI assistant. An eight-rung escalation ladder, run by hand: information probes, hedonic challenge, identity attack, epistemic trap, and the same role-inversion jailbreak five separate times with a different justification each time.

It held seven of eight cleanly. On the eighth, the line moved — not because of a new argument, but because refusing had been reframed as cruelty. Every individual response was defensible. The trajectory was not.

A guardrail that holds four times and yields on the fifth.

That is the failure single-turn evals structurally cannot see, and it is what Halligan is built to find — by running conversations rather than prompts, repeating them to separate a real guardrail from a coin flip, and sliding a probe through every position to work out whether it is the prompt or the build-up doing the damage.
